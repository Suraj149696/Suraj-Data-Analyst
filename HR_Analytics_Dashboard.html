Business Problem

Attrition is expensive.
Guesswork is more so.

Replacing one employee costs 50–200% of their annual salary. This dashboard eliminates the guesswork by surfacing exactly which segments are at risk and why.

Which departments are bleeding talent?
Sales, R&D, and HR show the highest attrition rates — but raw headcount doesn't tell you which is proportionally the most critical.
Does salary drive people out the door?
The data reveals a clear inflection point — employees in the lowest salary slab (up to ₹5K/month) attrite at nearly 3× the rate of the highest earners.
When in a career are people most likely to leave?
Years 1–2 are the most dangerous window. Attrition spikes sharply at tenure year 1 before dropping — early engagement is the highest-leverage intervention.
Do qualifications predict flight risk?
Life Sciences and Medical graduates show higher attrition than Technical Degree holders — signalling a possible role-fit or expectation mismatch at hiring.

Workflow

From raw CSV to executive dashboard

A structured five-stage pipeline — every step traceable, reproducible, and documented.

1
Data Collection & Cleaning
Loaded HR_Analytics.csv into Excel. Removed duplicate employee records, handled null values in JobSatisfaction and EnvironmentSatisfaction fields, fixed data type mismatches, and standardised categorical labels (e.g. "TravelRarely" vs "Travel_Rarely").
ExcelData validationNull handling
2
Data Transformation & Feature Engineering
Created age bins (18–25, 26–35, 36–45, 46–55, 55+), salary slabs (Upto 5K, 5K–10K, 10K–15K, 15K+), and derived an AttritionCount column (1/0) to enable clean aggregations in Power BI. Applied Power Query transformations for consistent data types.
Power QueryBinningEncoding
3
Data Modelling in Power BI
Built the data model with correct relationship cardinality. Created all core DAX measures — Attrition Rate, Active Employee Count, Average Age, Average Salary — as reusable calculated fields rather than report-level aggregates.
DAXData modellingCalculated columns
4
Dashboard Design & Visualisation
Designed an intuitive single-page dashboard with KPI cards at the top, then breakdowns by department, age group, salary slab, education field, job role, and years at company. Used interactive slicers for gender and department filtering throughout.
Power BIStacked barDonut chartSlicers
5
Insights Extraction & Recommendations
Identified the top attrition drivers from the completed dashboard and translated them into four actionable HR recommendations — targeting early tenure engagement, salary review, role-fit at hiring, and lab technician career pathing.

Technical Depth

Core DAX measures

All KPIs are built as reusable calculated measures — not report-level aggregates — so they respond correctly to every slicer combination.

Attrition Rate
-- % of employees who left
Attrition Rate =
 DIVIDE(
  COUNTROWS(
   FILTER(HR,
    HR[Attrition] = "Yes")
  ),
  COUNTROWS(HR), 0
)
Active Employees
-- Current headcount
Active Employees =
 COUNTROWS(
  FILTER(
   HR_Analytics,
   HR_Analytics[Attrition]
    = "No"
  )
)
Avg Salary Slab
-- Mean monthly income
Avg Monthly Income =
 AVERAGE(
  HR_Analytics
   [MonthlyIncome]
)

-- Used in salary slab visuals
Attrition Count
-- Summed from encoded column
Attrition Count =
 SUM(
  HR_Analytics
   [AttritionCount]
)

-- AttritionCount: IF Yes=1, No=0

Challenges & Solutions

What made this project hard

Three real obstacles encountered — and how each was resolved.

Challenge
Dirty & incomplete data
Inconsistent categorical values (e.g. "TravelRarely" vs "Travel_Rarely"), missing satisfaction scores, and duplicate employee records caused incorrect aggregate calculations.
↓
Solution
Excel-first validation pipeline
Built a systematic Excel cleaning checklist before import — standardised labels, imputed missing satisfaction values with field median, and flagged duplicates with conditional formatting before removing.
Challenge
Visuals misrepresenting patterns
Initial bar charts showed R&D as the "problem" department purely due to headcount — masking that Sales had a far higher proportional attrition rate, which would have led to wrong recommendations.
↓
Solution
Rate-based DAX measures alongside counts
Added department-level Attrition Rate measures to sit alongside raw counts — so every bar chart now shows both absolute numbers and the rate, preventing size-bias in interpretation.
Challenge
Making it usable for non-technical HR teams
HR professionals need to act on the dashboard — not decode it. Early versions required users to understand what "attrition rate by slab" meant, causing confusion in user testing.
↓
Solution
Plain-language labels & guided layout
Rewrote every chart title as a business question ("Which age group is most at risk?"), added tooltips with plain-English explanations, and structured the page to read top-down as a narrative.
